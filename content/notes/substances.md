## Psychedelics

### Weed (Marijuana) 🌿

- Forms: Typically found as dried leaves and flowers. Processed into edibles, oils, or concentrates.
- Common Ways of Taking: Smoking (joints, blunts, pipes), vaporising (bongs), edibles (ingestion), tinctures, or topicals.
- Chemical Formula: C21H30O2
- Effects on Neurons: Primarily affects neurons releasing neurotransmitters like dopamine. THC binds to cannabinoid receptors (CB1 and CB2) in the brain, inhibiting the release of neurotransmitters, leading to altered mood, perception, and coordination.
- Positive Effects: Euphoria, relaxation, altered sensory perception, increased creativity.
- Negative Effects: Short-term memory impairment, altered judgment, impaired coordination, anxiety, paranoia.
- Duration of Effects: Varies by method but typically lasts 1-3 hours.
- Overdose: While marijuana overdose is rare, excessive consumption can lead to adverse effects like anxiety, paranoia, increased heart rate.
- Short-term Health Risks: Impaired memory and concentration, altered judgment, respiratory issues (if smoked).
- Long-term Health Risks: Respiratory problems (from smoking), potential impact on mental health (association with anxiety, depression), dependence.
- Addiction Potential: Considered less addictive than substances like nicotine or alcohol. Approximately 9% of users may develop a dependence.

### Mushrooms (Psilocybin) 🍄

- Forms: Typically consumed in their natural state as dried or fresh mushrooms. Can also be ground into a powder or brewed into a tea.
- Common Ways of Taking: Ingested orally, often eaten directly or incorporated into food. Rarely smoked or injected.
- Chemical Formula: C12H17N2O4P
- Effects on Neurons: Psilocybin is converted to psilocin in the body, which acts as a partial agonist at serotonin receptors, leading to an increased release of serotonin.
- Positive Effects: Altered sensory perception, enhanced creativity, a sense of interconnectedness, and spiritual experiences.
- Negative Effects: Anxiety, nausea, confusion, hallucinations, potential for a “bad trip.”
- Duration of Effects: Typically 4-6 hours, with the peak occurring within the first few hours.
- Overdose: Overdosing on psilocybin-containing mushrooms is rare, but excessive consumption can lead to intense and potentially distressing experiences.
- Short-term Health Risks: Anxiety, nausea, hallucinations, impaired judgment, risk of accidents due to altered perception.
- Long-term Health Risks: No clear evidence of long-term physical harm, but persistent psychosis or HPPD (Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder) can occur in rare cases.
- Addiction Potential: Not considered addictive; tolerance develops rapidly, and its use doesn’t lead to physical dependence. Users typically do not experience cravings between uses.

### LSD (Acid) 🧬

- Forms: Typically found as colorless, odorless liquid, or as blotter paper squares infused with the liquid. Rarely encountered in crystalline form.
- Common Ways of Taking: Ingested orally, usually on blotter paper. Can also be found as a liquid, gelatin squares, or microdots. Rarely snorted or injected.
- Chemical Formula: C20H25N3O
- Effects on Neurons: LSD stimulates serotonin receptors (5-HT2A) in the brain, leading to an increased release of serotonin, causing altered mood, perception, and cognition.
- Positive Effects: Altered sensory perception, enhanced creativity, a sense of interconnectedness, and profound self-awareness.
- Negative Effects: Anxiety, paranoia, hallucinations, altered sense of time, potentially triggering latent mental health conditions.
- Duration of Effects: Typically 6-12 hours, with the peak occurring within the first few hours.
- Overdose: LSD overdose is rare, but it can lead to intense, prolonged effects. Extreme doses can result in psychological distress or even psychosis.
- Short-term Health Risks: Anxiety, paranoia, hallucinations, impaired judgment, risk of accidents due to altered perception.
- Long-term Health Risks: No clear evidence of long-term physical harm, but persistent psychosis or HPPD (Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder) can occur in rare cases.
- Addiction Potential: Not considered addictive; tolerance develops rapidly, and its use doesn’t lead to physical dependence. It is more of a psychologically dependent substance, with users typically not craving it between uses.

### MDMA (Ecstasy) 😇

- Forms: Usually appears as a crystalline powder or in tablet form, often with various logos or designs.
- Common Ways of Taking: Orally consumed as a pill or powder, occasionally snorted.
- Chemical Formula: C11H15NO2
- Effects on Neurons: Primarily affects serotonin, dopamine, and norepinephrine neurons, enhancing their release and blocking reuptake, resulting in heightened mood, increased energy, and altered perception.
- Positive Effects: Euphoria, increased empathy, enhanced sensory perception.
- Negative Effects: Dehydration, nausea, anxiety, jaw clenching, insomnia.
- Duration: Effects typically last 3 to 6 hours.
- Overdose: Can lead to hyperthermia, dehydration, seizures, and in severe cases, death.
- Short-term Risks: Dehydration, overheating, anxiety, paranoia, serotonin syndrome (rare but serious).
- Long-term Risks: Potential neurotoxicity to serotonin neurons, cognitive deficits with frequent use.
- Addiction Potential: MDMA is known for low physical dependence, but psychological dependence can occur. About 10% of MDMA users may develop dependency. Regular use may lead to tolerance and craving.

## Depressants

### Xanax (Benzodiazepines) 💊

- Forms: Typically in pill form, various colors, shapes, and dosages.
- Common Ways of Taking: Orally consumed as a pill.
- Chemical Formula: C17H13ClN4
- Effects on Neurons: Increases the activity of gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA), an inhibitory neurotransmitter, leading to calming effects and reduced neuronal excitability.
- Positive Effects: Anxiolysis (anxiety reduction), sedation, muscle relaxation.
- Negative Effects: Drowsiness, dizziness, impaired coordination, memory issues.
- Duration: Effects typically last a few hours.
- Overdose: Can cause extreme sedation, respiratory depression, and, in severe cases, death.
- Short-term Risks: Drowsiness, impaired coordination, potential for addiction.
- Long-term Risks: Risk of dependence, withdrawal symptoms, cognitive impairment.
- Addiction Potential: Xanax is considered highly addictive, with a potential for physical and psychological dependence. Approximately 20-30% of users may develop a dependence.

### Nicotine 🚬

- Forms: Commonly found in tobacco products such as cigarettes, cigars, and chewing tobacco. Also available in e-cigarettes or vaping devices.
- Common Ways of Taking: Smoking (cigarettes, cigars, pipes), chewing tobacco, using nicotine patches, or vaping.
- Chemical Formula: C10H14N2
- Effects on Neurons: Binds to nicotinic acetylcholine receptors, leading to increased release of dopamine and other neurotransmitters, resulting in heightened mood and improved attention.
- Positive Effects: Increased alertness, improved mood, enhanced focus and concentration.
- Negative Effects: Nausea, dizziness, increased heart rate, potential addiction.
- Duration of Effects: Nicotine's effects are relatively short-lived, typically lasting a short duration, leading to frequent use.
- Overdose: Nicotine overdose is possible, leading to symptoms like nausea, increased heart rate, and in severe cases, seizures.
- Short-term Health Risks: Increased heart rate, elevated blood pressure, nausea, potential for addiction.
- Long-term Health Risks: Increased risk of cardiovascular diseases, respiratory issues, addiction-related complications.
- Addiction Potential: Approximately 32-45% of individuals who use nicotine-containing tobacco products develop a dependence. The addictive potential is often compared to substances like heroin or cocaine.

### Cocaine ❄️

- Forms: Typically found as a white powder or crystalline substance. Cocaine hydrochloride is the most common form. Can be converted to a freebase (crack) for smoking.
- Common Ways of Taking: Snorting, smoking (freebasing or crack cocaine), or injecting.
- Chemical Formula: C17H21NO4
- Effects on Neurons: Cocaine inhibits the reuptake of neurotransmitters, especially dopamine, by binding to the transporter protein, preventing the reabsorption of dopamine back into the presynaptic neuron. This lead to an accumulation of neurotransmitters in the synaptic space, intensifying their effects.
- Positive Effects: Euphoria, increased energy, heightened alertness.
- Negative Effects: Anxiety, paranoia, increased heart rate, risk of cardiovascular issues, potential for addiction.
- Duration of Effects: Short-lived, typically 15-30 minutes, leading to a cycle of repeated use.
- Overdose: Overdosing on cocaine can lead to severe cardiovascular issues, seizures, and death.
- Short-term Health Risks: Increased heart rate, elevated blood pressure, anxiety, risk of heart attack or stroke, potential for overdose.
- Long-term Health Risks: Cardiovascular issues, respiratory problems, neurological effects, mental health issues, addiction-related complications.
- Addiction Potential: Highly addictive; about 20-25% of individuals who use cocaine develop a dependence, making it one of the most addictive substances.

### Methamphetamine 🧊

- Forms: Typically found as a white crystalline powder or in crystal form (crystal meth).
- Common Ways of Taking: Orally ingested, snorted, smoked, or injected.
- Chemical Formula: C10H15N
- Effects on Neurons: Increases the release of neurotransmitters, especially dopamine, and inhibits their reuptake, leading to an accumulation in the synaptic cleft.
- Positive Effects: Increased energy, euphoria, enhanced focus.
- Negative Effects: Anxiety, paranoia, increased heart rate, risk of cardiovascular issues, potential for addiction.
- Duration of Effects: Longer-lasting than cocaine, typically 6-12 hours.
- Overdose: Overdosing on methamphetamine can lead to severe cardiovascular issues, hyperthermia, and death.
- Short-term Health Risks: Increased heart rate, elevated blood pressure, insomnia, risk of heart attack or stroke, potential for overdose.
- Long-term Health Risks: Cardiovascular issues, dental problems ("meth mouth"), neurological effects, mental health issues, addiction-related complications.
- Addiction Potential: Highly addictive; about 15-20% of individuals who use methamphetamine develop a dependence. The repeated use and rapid development of tolerance contribute to its addictive nature.

## Opioids/Narcotics

### Morphine Ⓜ️

- Forms: Typically as a white powder or as a solution.
- Common Ways of Taking: Orally, injection, or intravenous administration.
- Chemical Formula: C17H19NO3
- Effects on Neurons: Binds to μ-opioid receptors, inhibiting the release of neurotransmitters like substance P, resulting in pain relief and euphoria.
- Positive Effects: Pain relief, euphoria, sedation.
- Negative Effects: Drowsiness, constipation, nausea, respiratory depression.
- Duration: Effects typically last a few hours.
- Overdose: Excessive intake can lead to respiratory depression, coma, and death.
- Short-term Risks: Respiratory depression, constipation, potential for addiction.
- Long-term Risks: Risk of dependence, tolerance, constipation.
- Addiction Potential: Morphine is highly addictive. Continuous use can lead to physical and psychological dependence. Approximately 10-15% of users develop a dependence.

### Heroin 💉

- Forms: Typically found as a white or brown powder or a black, sticky substance (black tar heroin). Commonly dissolved in water for injection.
- Common Ways of Taking: Injected, snorted, or smoked. Rarely ingested orally.
- Chemical Formula: C21H23NO5
- Effects on Neurons: Heroin converts to morphine in the brain and binds to opioid receptors, reducing the release of neurotransmitters, especially dopamine. Slowing down neural activity and dampening excitatory signals, it leads to a depressant effect on the central nervous system.
- Positive Effects: Euphoria, pain relief, feelings of warmth and relaxation.
- Negative Effects: Drowsiness, slowed breathing, nausea, risk of respiratory failure, potential for addiction.
- Duration of Effects: Relatively short-lived, typically a few hours.
- Overdose: Overdosing on heroin can lead to respiratory failure, unconsciousness, and death. Symptoms: slow and shallow breathing, blue lips and fingernails, clammy skin, convulsions, coma.
- Short-term Health Risks: Respiratory depression, sedation, risk of overdose, potential for HIV or hepatitis transmission through injection.
- Long-term Health Risks: Addiction, collapsed veins, abscesses, increased risk of infectious diseases, overdose-related complications.
- Addiction Potential: Highly addictive; about 23-30% of individuals who use heroin develop a dependence. The intense euphoria and rapid onset contribute to its addictive nature.

### Fentanyl 👧🏻

- Forms: White powder; commonly consumed by injection, nasal spray, or patch absorption.
- Common Ways of Taking: Injection, nasal spray, absorption through patches.
- Chemical Formula: C22H28N2O
- Effects on Neurons: Binds to opioid receptors, inhibits neurotransmitter release, especially dopamine, leading to potent analgesic and depressant effect on the central nervous system.
- Lipophilic, so can quickly cross through the fatty blood-brain barrier, a shield that usually stops drugs from getting to the brain.
- Positive Effects: Powerful relief, sedation. Warmth
- Negative Effects: Respiratory depression, nausea, confusion, potential for addiction.
- Duration: Short-lived, typically 1-2 hours.
- Overdose: Small amounts can be lethal; symptoms include respiratory distress, coma, and death.
- Short-term Risks: Respiratory issues, sedation, overdose risk.
- Long-term Risks: Addiction, respiratory problems, tolerance.
- Addiction Potential: Highly addictive; estimated to be 50 to 100 times more potent than morphine. Approximately 20-30% of individuals who misuse fentanyl may develop dependency.

## Harm Reduction

### Naloxone

- Reverse overdoses
- Receptor antagonist
  - Binds to mu-opioid receptors without activating them; blocks other opioids from binding.
- Needle or nasal spray
- Can restore normal breathing in minutes
