### What each ingredient does

*Think of Basque cheesecake as a rich baked custard: eggs set the mixture; cream cheese supplies most of its body; cream softens it; starch binds water; sugar sweetens and delays firm setting.*

#### Cream cheese
- **More:**  
  - denser, thicker, richer, tangier
  - more cheesecake-like, less fluid
  - w rest fixed, cake sets firmer
- **Less:**  
  - lighter, softer, less tangy
  - w same cream/eggs, cake more diluted, may feel loose or custardy.

Main source of dairy solids, fat and characteristic cheesecake flavour. Full-fat cream cheese gives the richest result.

#### Cream
- **More:**  
  - softer, silkier, more fluid, creamier
  - dilutes the cheese flavour
  - too much relative to the eggs/cheese can make the centre weak or almost loose
- **Less:**  
  - thicker, denser, more cheese-forward
  - usually firmer, with less of the soft custard quality

Contributes fat and water, but relatively little structure. It does not simply make the cake richer: its water also needs to be held by the setting ingredients.

#### Eggs
- **More:**  
  - stronger set, more structure; firmer, more custard-like/springy slice
  - can taste eggy and become rubbery if overbaked
- **Less:**  
  - softer, more fragile, potentially runnier centre
  - less ability to hold shape after cooling

Whole eggs provide both setting proteins and water. 
- More whites → firmer, more elastic set.
- Extra yolk → richer, silkier, more tender result

Egg proteins coagulate with heat. The centre continues setting as it cools.

#### Cornstarch
- **More:**  
  - thicker batter and a more stable, sliceable set
  - helps bind water, reduces risk of watery or overly loose centre
  - too much can make the texture pasty, gummy or slightly powdery, and mute the creamy mouthfeel
- **Less / none:**  
  - more delicate, custardy, potentially flowing texture
  - less insurance against a loose or weeping centre

Starch is optional in many Basque-style recipes. It is not the main setting agent: eggs and dairy proteins still matter most.

#### Sugar
- **More:**  
  - more caramelised/browned top
  - softer set and more tender texture (cuz sugar competes for water and delays protein setting)
  - excess can make it overly sweet, sticky or too soft
- **Less:**  
  - less browning; top may be less deeply coloured
  - somewhat firmer/earlier set

Sugar affects texture and browning as well as sweetness.

### Ingredient combinations and balance
- **More cream + fewer eggs:** especially soft, creamy and potentially under-set. A longer bake may firm it, but can overcook the outside first.
- **More cream cheese + fewer cream:** dense, rich, tangy and firm; can become heavy rather than silky.
- **More eggs + less starch:** a cleaner custard-like set, but greater risk of an eggy or rubbery texture if baked too far.
- **More starch + more cream:** starch can help hold the extra water, but too much starch still makes the crumb pasty rather than truly creamy.
- **More sugar + longer/hotter bake:** faster/deeper browning and stronger bitter-caramel notes; the top can become acrid if pushed too far.
- Change one variable at a time when experimenting. Keep the pan size, batter depth, ingredient temperature and bake conditions constant; otherwise it is hard to tell what caused the difference.

### Oven temperature and baking time

#### Higher temperature
- The surface browns and caramelises faster; gives the signature dark top and stronger toasted/bitter-sweet flavour.
- Creates a bigger temperature difference between the browned outside and soft centre: sides set while the middle can remain creamy and wobbly.
- Too high for too long can burn the top before the centre reaches the desired set; the outer ring may become dry or overcooked.
- A very hot oven is characteristic of Basque cheesecake, but exact settings depend on oven, fan, pan size and batter depth.

#### Lower temperature
- Slower, gentler and more even cooking; generally less dramatic browning and a paler, less bitter top.
- Gives the centre more time to warm and set before the outside becomes very dark, but a long bake can still dry the cake.
- Useful when the top is colouring too quickly or the cake is unusually deep; it will not produce the same dark crust as a short, very hot bake.

#### Longer baking time
- More egg/dairy proteins coagulate: the centre moves from fluid → custardy → firmly set.
- Less wobble, cleaner slices and a firmer texture.
- Too long: dry, dense, grainy or rubbery outer texture; centre loses its creamy quality. More browning can become bitterness/burnt flavour.
- The cake will continue to set as it cools, and chilling firms it further. Do not bake until it looks fully firm in the oven if you want a creamy centre.

#### Shorter baking time
- Softer, looser and more flowing centre; less browning and less roasted flavour.
- Too short: centre may be liquid rather than creamy, and the cake may not hold its shape once cooled.
- Judge by the centre's wobble, not just the clock: a broad wobble is expected; a sloshing/liquid centre is usually underbaked.

### Temperature + time together
- **Hotter + shorter:** strongest top colour and contrast between dark exterior and soft centre; easiest to leave a creamy middle, but watch for a burnt surface.
- **Cooler + longer:** more even set and usually less intense top colour; easier to end up with a uniformly firm cake if baked too long.
- **Hotter + longer:** darkest top and firmest outer ring, but highest risk of bitter burning and dry/rubbery edges.
- **Cooler + shorter:** pale top and soft centre; highest chance of an under-set cake.

These are trends, not fixed rules. A deeper cake needs more time than a shallow one; fan ovens transfer heat more aggressively and often need a lower set temperature.

---

### Practical cues
- For a classic Basque contrast, aim for a deeply browned top, set/puffed edges and a centre that still wobbles noticeably when the pan is shaken.
- Cool completely before judging the final texture. Refrigeration makes it firmer; let it warm slightly before serving if you want the centre to taste softer.
- Use the same pan and fill depth for comparisons. Record oven mode (fan/conventional), rack position, temperature, time and cooling/chilling time.
- The current 300 g cream cheese / 150 g cream / 2 eggs / 60 g sugar / 10 g cornstarch formula is a useful baseline. Adjust one ingredient at a time and note both the oven wobble and the fully cooled texture.
