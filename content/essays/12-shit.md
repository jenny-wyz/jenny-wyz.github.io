This is where the mind wanders on a sleepless night at 5:39 a.m.

A tale about my depression (2022–2023)

It's a big pile of messy context. I think there are two factors:
- interpersonal (the trigger, the beginning), and
- intrapersonal (the deterioration).

#### Intrapersonal - Meaning and Nihilism

I lowkey hate the word "meaning" nowadays. I used to be obsessed with the meaning of life (during depression), and that obsession ended up constraining my mind and making things worse.

I started wondering about tmol in grade 9 or 10, around the same time I began thinking more about death. (This was also when my interest in philosophy first bloomed.) Back then, it was more of a leisure activity. I didn't really "overthink" (ruminate). Eventually, I concluded that I wasn't able to come up with an answer, so I gave up and focused on other things.

```
Life ends. It could end later; it could end now.
One could keep it (default)
At any moment it could end (passive)
At every moment one could end it (active decision)
There's no difference between dying today and tomorrow (or in 50 years), cuz after death, there's no difference
There has to be a meaning to it for one to keep it today then!  (incentive, motive)
```

Whenever I'm in an extended emo session, I have this (turns out, not-so-benevolent) habit of throwing myself into philosophy (sufficient condition, not necessary condition). Partly, it's a way to escape my own real-world problems and/or mental angst; partly, I genuinely want to find an answer, or at least some kind of solution in life. Existential philosophy in particular, because of course, mol.

Enough background.

I did exactly this soon after my depressed mood became impossible for my brain to ignore or suppress. (Summer in London.) (Kierkegaard, Senegal, Schopenhauer.) The other major form of distraction I turned to was travelling. When I got back to Vancouver, my mood lifted a little at first (a change of location, perhaps, and an unconscious hope that my mental state would change with it). But it didn't last long. Before I knew it, I had sunk even lower than before.

I was really unhappy. I lost all my hobbies, all my motivation, and eventually, the ability to find joy in anything. All I could think about, and all I tried to do, was figuring out how to be happy. I became obsessed with finding out tmol; I was desperate for an answer. (Did I think that knowing the answer would somehow make me happy?) I kept reading: Sartre, Nietzsche (bits and pieces), Camus, etc. But none of it gave me a satisfactory answer. (I was quite fond of Camus's absurdism, but I couldn't fathom why one should choose rebellion over suicide.)

It was a malicious cycle.

The deeper I dug -> the less satisfied I became, the more desperate I became, the more emo I felt -> the deeper I dug -> ...

And with every turn, the helplessness and hopelessness seemed to grow a little more.

Whenever I was left alone with my brain, I had to face the fact that I was unhappy, that my life felt filled with suffering. Every conscious second felt like suffering, like torment. Every action I took was an attempt to momentarily lift myself out of my own head. But I knew these efforts were temporary, almost futile: I couldn't switch my brain off, and the suffering underneath was still there when the distraction faded. Back then, it seemed to me that there was no escape from it. It became perfectly logical in my mind: as long as I was conscious, I would be unhappy (I didn't believe I would ever be happy again); as long as I was alive, I would suffer.

What's the point of holding on to a life in which happiness feels impossible, when all that seems to remain is endless, inextricable suffering?
