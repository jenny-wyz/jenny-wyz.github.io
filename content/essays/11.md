This is where the mind goes on a sleepless night at 5:39 a.m.

A tale about my depression (2022–2023)

It's a big pile of messy context. I discern two factors:
- interpersonal (the trigger, the start) and
- intrapersonal (the deterioration).

#### Intrapersonal - Meaning and Nihilism

I lowkey hate the word "meaning" nowadays. I used to be obsessed with the meaning of life (during depression), and that's what constrained my mind and exacerbated it.

I started wondering about tmol in grade 9 or 10, when I also started thinking more about death. (This was also the time when my interest in philosophy bloomed.) It was more of a leisure activity, and at that time I did not "overthink" (ruminate). After concluding that I was not able to come up with an answer, I gave up and focused on other things.

```
Life ends. It could end later; it could end now.
One could keep it (default)
At any moment it could end (passive)
At every moment one could end it (active decision)
There's no difference between dying today and tomorrow (or in 50 years), cuz after death, there's no difference
There has to be a meaning to it for one to keep it today then!  (incentive, motive)
```

Whenever I am in an extended emo session, I have a (turns out not-so-benevolent) habit of throwing myself into philosophy (sufficient condition, not necessary condition) -- 1 as a form of escape from my own real-world problems and/or mental angst, and 2 to genuinely seek an answer and/or solution. Existential philosophy, particularly (because of course, mol).

Enough background.

I did exactly this soon after my depressed mood became impossible for my brain to ignore or suppress. (Summer in London.) (Kierkegaard, Senegal, Schopenhauer.) (The other major form of distraction I was using was travelling.) When I got back to Vancouver, my mood went up a bit initially (change of location, probably unconsciously hoping for a change of mental state), but it quickly dropped even lower than before.

I was really unhappy; I lost all hobbies, all motivation; I lost the ability to find joy in anything. All I could think about and try to do was how to be happy. I became obsessed with finding out tmol; I was desperate for an answer. (Did I think I would become happy once I knew the answer?) I kept reading: Sartre, Nietzsche (bits and pieces), Camus, etc. But I did not get a satisfactory answer at all. (I was quite fond of Camus's absurdism, but could not fathom why one should choose rebellion over suicide.)

It was a malicious cycle.\
The deeper I dig -> the less satisfied I became, the more desperate I became, the more emo I was -> the deeper I dig -> ...\
Total helplessness and hopelessness grew in me.

Whenever I was left alone with my brain, I was forced to face the fact that I was unhappy and that my life was filled with suffering. Every conscious second was suffering, was torment. Every action I took was an attempt to temporarily lift me out of my brain, but I realized that all these efforts were temporary and futile, since I was ultimately unable to shut down my brain, and the underlying suffering persisted -- unless I shut down my brain forever, for good. It became perfectly logical to me: as long as I was conscious, I would be unhappy (I didn't believe I would ever be happy again); as long as I was alive, I would suffer.

What's the point of keeping a life in which there is no hope for happiness, but only endless and inextricable suffering?
1. I will die one day
2. When I die, the suffering will be gone. [null]
3. When I am alive (rn), I suffer. [negative]
4. [positive > null > negative]
5. Happiness is an impossibility. [positive is an impossibility]
6. [null > negative]
7. die now = end the indefinite negatives asap, set all to null
8. voila, problem solved, im a genius
